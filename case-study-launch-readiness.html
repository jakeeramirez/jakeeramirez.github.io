Case Study 01 · Product × CX Operating Model

Making Customer Experience a Partner in Product Development

Designing an operating model that connected Product development, launch readiness, and customer feedback across 63 launches, 4 product domains, and 10 squads.

ROLEProgram Manager, CX PMO & Strategic Projects
FOCUSOperating Model · Product Partnership · Launch Readiness · VoC
63launches

01
The challenge

Product launches were happening without a consistent operating model between Product and Customer Experience.

Sometimes CX learned about launches too late to prepare. Other times, collaboration depended on individual relationships, Slack messages, and tribal knowledge of who to involve. The result was inconsistent CX involvement and an inability to effectively support customers through product changes.

Build a scalable, two-way operating model between Product and CX without adding unnecessary process or requiring CX to embed everywhere.
BEFORE

Ad hoc and reactive

  • Relationships and tribal knowledge drove involvement
  • CX often entered late
  • Launch information lived across Slack and individual teams
  • Customer insight was mostly reactive
  • Readiness varied by launch
AFTER

Proactive and structured

  • A defined engagement model
  • CX proactively engaged based on impact
  • Centralized visibility across launches
  • Customer sentiment could inform product development requirements
  • Clear ownership and readiness standards
02
The insight

Product development was fluid. The CX model needed to fit into it, not slow it down.

Rather than create a parallel process, I joined existing Product rhythms and built continuous visibility across the roadmap. CX involvement could then scale with the customer and operational impact of the work.

Maintain visibility everywhere. Concentrate CX where customer and operational impact are justified.

The engagement model

Partner where CX can shape the outcome. Consult where context adds value. Execute where readiness is the priority.

PARTNER

Embedded CX partnership

Highest-impact work. CX participates from development through launch and post-launch learning.

CONSULT

Targeted involvement

Continuous roadmap visibility, key development touchpoints, and coordinated readiness.

EXECUTE

Standardized readiness

Lightweight intake gives CX the context and lead time needed to execute.

COMMON FOUNDATION → PRODUCT ROADMAP VISIBILITY

03
The system

Turn the framework into something teams could actually operate.

Dedicated partnerships

CX Program Managers aligned to Product squads, creating known counterparts instead of relationship-dependent routing.

Standardized intake

Product and Product Marketing adopted a defined path into CX with clear information requirements.

Portfolio visibility

A centralized Asana portfolio surfaced current and upcoming launches, timelines, readiness, and capacity implications.

Repeatable execution

Templates and rhythms coordinated Content, Training, Delivery, and other CX functions.

The closed loop

Customer insight→Product development→CX readiness→Launch→Measure & learn↺

For Partner launches, CX could bring customer and operational data into requirements and PRDs before launch, then evaluate customer reception through CSAT, contact volume, frontline feedback, and launch-specific metrics afterward.

04
What changed

A repeatable organizational capability replaced ad hoc coordination.

  • Adoption: Product and Product Marketing adhered to standardized CX intake requirements.
  • Visibility: CX and leadership gained a consolidated roadmap of launches, timelines, readiness work, and capacity needs.
  • Earlier influence: In Partner work, CX was involved early enough to bring customer and operational context into requirements, PRDs, and solution design.
  • Operational readiness: Content and Training received complete inputs against agreed preparation windows of approximately 1 to 3 weeks rather than piecemeal updates.
  • Closed-loop learning: Post-launch customer and operational signals could return to Product to inform iteration.

Takeaway

Standardize the system, not the level of effort.

Customer Experience doesn’t need to be involved in every Product decision. It needs an operating model that puts customer intelligence and operational expertise into the decisions where they create the most value.
